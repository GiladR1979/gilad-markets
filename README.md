# Gilad Markets

A public dashboard of closed and settled returns on Gilad’s Polymarket trading account. The dashboard opens on the whole account. Its bot filter shows ledger-matched trades across deployments. Starting with `Successful8`, each numbered Git tag starts a reporting period when its commit or a descendant first reaches the bot. Subsequent deployments belong to that period until the next numbered tag reaches the bot. The publisher reads tags from GitHub and checks deployment times in the live ledger. On the publisher host, `.local/release-source.txt` can name a read-only Git checkout for verifying deployed commits, including unpublished ones. GitHub supplies ancestry when local Git history is unavailable. The static site is served from `docs/` on GitHub Pages.

Each row groups the outcomes held in a single market. A deal qualifies once all remaining balances fall below 0.01 shares, or when the CLOB and Gamma market records agree on final token payouts. The result starts with reported realized P&L. At settlement, each remaining open balance contributes its payout less its remaining cost including entry fees. Provider-closed legs already include their result. The snapshot is rebuilt per market, so redemption cannot add a second deal. All-time account figures include manual activity. A market receives a release tag when all its lifetime purchases match confirmed bot buys and all originating pairs opened in the same release period. The tag stays with the market through settlement. Mixed-period and unmatched markets remain available in the broader views.

The dashboard also counts a provisional loss when a held outcome of at least 0.01 shares trades at 1% or less of its average entry price and the combined market P&L is negative. The calculation includes realized P&L on every leg, subtracts the remaining cost including fees, values filled complementary shares at $1 per pair, and marks excess shares at their current price. An unfilled order contributes no shares. The snapshot keeps these observations in `provisional` with a mark time. A recovery removes the observation, and a final result takes its place in `deals` on closure. The public totals, outcome counts, chart and ledger include each market once.

The publisher reconciles wallet activity with the provider's final share balances and P&L before assigning an economic close time. A sale or merge that takes every outcome below 0.01 shares closes the market at that event. A later material purchase reopens it. If material shares remain until resolution, the close uses the verified payout time or an earlier completed redemption. Later dust disposal and redemption can update the final cash amount. The deal retains its economic `closed` time and shows the later `bookedAt` time separately. When the history cannot be reconciled, the publisher keeps the provider-based time. The publisher caches confirmed final payouts locally and rechecks unresolved markets.

Every counted deal gets an inline chart and mood portrait during the scheduled refresh. The exporter reconciles public account activity with the position, then estimates its open P&L from cash flows, holdings and historical market prices. It publishes compact samples and leaves breaks where prices are missing. For a nonnegative close with sufficient history, the largest peak-to-trough drop as a percentage of total dollars invested determines the portrait. The cutoffs are 5% and 15%. A loss always gets the crying portrait, and a nonnegative close with insufficient history gets the neutral portrait.

The APY portrait follows the matching closed deals across all ledger pages and the selected chart window. The period starts at the latest verified equity observation before the earliest selected entry and ends at the final selected close. The starting bankroll includes cash and marked positions. With no external funding during the period, the observed return divides the selected deals’ net P&L by that bankroll and compounds it over a 365-day year.

For a period with deposits or withdrawals, the portrait uses a money-weighted annual return. It solves for the rate at which the opening equity and each dated transfer would grow to the opening balance plus net transfers plus the selected deals’ P&L. The period return is the equivalent growth factor over the actual elapsed time. Trade purchases, sales and redemptions are internal activity. The publisher verifies the wallet's paginated deposit and withdrawal history and exports signed USD totals by timestamp within the supported deal periods. The calculation details show the funding total along with P&L. Missing funding evidence or an indeterminate rate is identified on the card.

Opening equity comes from complete wallet-matched account snapshots in the read-only bot ledger. For the first historical entry, the publisher can also use its host-local `.local/verified-opening-equity-v1.json` attestation of collateral and outcome balances from the preceding block. Only the measured USD equity and timestamp are exported. A selection whose opening equity cannot be verified shows the reason on the card.

The browser receives market names, localizable timestamps, results and derived chart data. The host-side exporter opens SQLite read-only and reads both public position views. The publisher checks every 15 minutes, updates the snapshot when results or histories change, and refreshes its timestamp at least every six hours while the host and data services are available. GitHub Pages may take a few minutes to serve a new version.

The ledger orders negative results by the earliest verified 99% price-loss observation. Historical replay uses the average entry, filled hedge and net market value at the time of each observation. The publisher also saves live observations in its local SQLite file so refreshes and redemption retain the first timestamp. The public `crashAt` field records this time separately from `closed` and `markedAt`. The cumulative chart books the measured crash P&L at that observation, then any difference at the economic close or latest mark. When only the crash time is known, the chart books the amount at its verified endpoint.

### Moderating comments

Visitors post in the [public comment thread](https://github.com/GiladR1979/gilad-markets/issues/1). To approve a reply for the dashboard, react to that reply with 👍 from the repository owner's account. Remove your reaction, hide the reply, or delete it to take it off the dashboard. If someone edits an approved comment, remove and reapply your 👍 after reviewing the new text. The same 15-minute publisher checks comment approvals independently of the trading snapshot. It writes the approved comment text, GitHub username and posting time to the public feed.
