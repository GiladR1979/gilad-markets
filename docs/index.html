      Data notes

      What counts as a deal?

      Each deal groups all outcomes held in one market. It counts as closed when every balance falls below 0.01 shares or Polymarket confirms the final payouts. Where the trade history reconciles, the close time shows when the position reached dust, exited, or settled. Otherwise the card uses the reported close. A later redemption or residual booking can update the final P&L. The card shows later bookings separately when their timing is known. All deal times use your local time zone.

      How is profit calculated?

      We add realized P&L across both sides of each deal. For shares still carried as open at confirmed settlement, we also include the final payout minus their remaining cost. Losing shares pay $0 even if they stay in the wallet. Each market counts once, including after redemption. The account total includes manual activity.

      How does the 99% loss rule work?

      An open position triggers the rule when its price falls to 1% or less of its average entry price. We count a provisional loss only if the whole market is underwater after costs and realized gains. Filled shares on opposite sides pair at $1 per complete set. Only the unmatched shares are valued at their current price. Pending orders provide no offset. Each refresh checks the rule again. A recovery removes the provisional loss, and a close replaces it with the final result.

      When is a loss shown?

      The ledger dates a losing deal to the first recorded 99% drop while the combined position was losing. Its blue chart stops there. The cumulative chart records the measured loss at that point and any difference at the close or latest mark. If the amount at the drop is unknown, the chart records the P&L at the close or latest mark. A market counts once within a chart period, including when it has multiple P&L changes.

      Which deals belong to each view?

      Entire account includes bot and manual activity. Ledger-matched bot includes markets whose purchases reconcile to the bot’s execution ledger across all deployments. Starting with Successful8, each numbered tag covers its deployment and later commits until the next numbered tag reaches the bot. A deal belongs to the period in which its bot pairs opened, even if it closes under a later tag. Markets with purchases from multiple periods stay in the broader views.

      What do the deal charts show?

      The blue line estimates P&L from account cash flows and historical Polymarket prices while the deal was open. Complementary shares are valued together at $1. For a recorded 99% crash, the diamond shows the estimated P&L at that moment when a price is available. Other charts end at the close or latest mark. Breaks in the line indicate missing prices. Hover, tap or use the arrow keys on a chart to inspect its samples.

      How is the mood chosen?

      A losing close gets the crying portrait. For other deals, we measure the largest fall from a running P&L high, starting at zero, as a percentage of the total dollars invested. A drop under 5% is Smooth, 5% to under 15% is Troubling, and 15% or more is Nerve-wracking. When too few prices are available to rate the ride, the portrait is neutral.

      How fresh is the data?

      A read-only publisher on the bot host checks every 15 minutes. Trade changes publish on the next successful check. Quiet periods receive a timestamp update every six hours. The footer shows the snapshot time.
